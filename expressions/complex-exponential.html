The story behind the Expression

Euler’s formula is the bridge

The key relationship is

ejθ = cos θ + j sin θ

where j is the imaginary unit and θ is an angle. Set θ = ωt and the expression describes a point rotating around the complex plane at angular frequency ω. Its magnitude stays at one while its phase advances with time. That rotating picture is the conceptual engine behind phasors and much of classical signal analysis.

Why engineers write j instead of i

Mathematicians normally use i for the imaginary unit. Electrical engineers commonly use j because i is already heavily used for electric current. Same number, different notation: j² = −1. That small convention is one of the reasons the Expression feels immediately native to electrical engineering rather than generic mathematics.

Why it matters

Sinusoids are special in linear time-invariant systems: feed in a sinusoid and, after transients, the output has the same frequency with its amplitude and phase changed. Complex exponentials make that behavior algebraically clean. Fourier analysis, filters, modulation, impedance, control systems, communications, and countless other tools become easier to reason about when oscillation is written this way.

Why wear it?

Because ejωt is a compact recognition signal. Someone who has spent time with circuits, signals, RF, controls, or communications does not need the hat to explain itself. They already know where it lives. That instant recognition is the point.
